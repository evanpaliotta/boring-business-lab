How an idea earns a score

6 factors · weighted 0–100

One scorecard, applied honestly to everything

The point of a fixed rubric is to stop the sexy-new-thing slide. Heavy AI, clever branding, or a viral thread cannot lift an idea whose buyers refuse to pay or whose suppliers are already full.

  • Demand proven 25%
  • Willingness to pay 20%
  • Supply/capacity gap 15%
  • Competitive openness 15%
  • Capital lightness 10%
  • My unfair advantage 15%
score = Σ(factor/5 × weight) × 100 → 0–100

Reading the number

70+ — worth spending money or a week of calls on. Not proof; permission to test.

50–69 — worth a cheap probe only: a few calls, one data pull, no build.

Under 50 — park it. Most ideas live here and that is fine.

Hard disqualifiers, regardless of score:

  • Every plausible supplier is booked out with no way to add capacity
  • Revenue requires reselling platform leads you do not own
  • The only evidence is a thread, not a buyer or a supplier
  • It needs inventory, staff, or a lease before the first dollar

Playbooks worth stealing

mechanics, stripped of hype
Arbitrage · lead flow

Sell the match, not the website

A city+service gap is only real when a buyer wants something a local supplier cannot deliver in time. Own the routing relationship, not a thin city page. Charge per qualified request or on close.

Fails when: the supplier is full, or ranking never comes.

Done-for-you · AI ops

Install an existing product, charge setup + monthly

The software is commoditised; distribution and trust are not. Sell an outcome (“70% of tickets answered”), install a configured agent, keep it tuned. Setup fee plus a monthly retainer is the cleanest cash-flow shape for a solo operator.

Fails when: you sell a tool instead of a result, or skip the maintain-and-report part.

Brokerage · rental

Broker capacity you do not own

Equipment, trucks, trailers, specialty crews. You are the availability layer: who has it, where, when, at what rate. Margin comes from coordination and trust, not from owning iron.

Fails when: you take a fee without verifying availability.

Media · niche

Narrow recurring information

One city, one obsession, one recurring trigger (deals, permits, prices, availability). Cheap to run, but the asset is the list and the habit, and both take months.

Fails when: the content is generic and the audience is “everyone”.
