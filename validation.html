Supplier script

7 questions · under 6 minutes · no pitch until #7

Say this, in this order

“Hi — I'm doing a small study on crane rental
coverage in Laredo, not selling anything today.
Can I ask you four quick things?”

1. If someone needed a crane next Tuesday,
   could you take the job — or are you booked?

2. What's the longest lead time you're seeing
   right now?

3. Do you get calls for work you can't take?
   What kind — and where does it go?

4. When you're full, is there anyone you
   refer people to?

Then, only if answers 1–3 sound open:
5. Would more inbound requests be useful,
   or would they be a nuisance?
6. If I brought you a qualified request,
   what would that be worth to you?
7. Who decides that, and what would you
   need to see from me first?

What counts as a signal

Go: "yes, send it" — and a number, even vague ("$50–100 a job", "a few hundred a month").

Maybe: "sure, but I'm busy" → ask what week is slow. Seasonal capacity is a real opening.

No: booked solid with no expansion plan, or refers work away already — that is your answer, and it's cheap to learn.

Do not open with "I can get you leads." It triggers the Angi reflex and you'll get a price objection before you understand their capacity.

Log everything: name, date, booked-or-open, lead time, what work they refuse, who they refer to, dollar figure, next step.

Buyer-side script

5 calls · same week

Suppliers tell you about capacity. Buyers tell you whether the problem is real. Call five that plausibly rent cranes or need septic work and ask about the last time they needed it:

“Last time you needed a crane / septic pump-out in Laredo —
how did you find someone, how long did it take to get a
callback, and did anyone not call you back?”

Follow-ups: What did the delay cost you? Did you call
someone out of town? Would you pay to have one number
that gets you an available operator today?

Good buyer sources: industrial contractors, warehouse operators, property managers, and the cross-border logistics firms around the World Trade Bridge — they rent lifting equipment with deadlines.

Kill criteria

decide before you call

Kill the crane idea if: 4 or more of 6 independents say they are booked out with no plan to add capacity, or nobody will name a dollar figure for a qualified job, or every one of them already gets more inbound than they can serve.

Kill the septic idea if: the two no-website operators turn out to be inactive/disconnected, or buyers say they get same-day service without trouble (the pain is fake), or the only operators who want leads are the ones already ranked #1 and don't need them.

Kill the whole model for this market if: both suppliers and buyers describe the problem as "we just call the guy we know." That means the relationship is the product and you have no wedge.

Honest caveats on the data

read before trusting any number

Keyword volume and CPC changed between providers. Serpstat reports crane rental at 9,900/mo and CPC $2.00; the earlier DataForSEO run reported $7.90 CPC and septic at 90,500 vs. Serpstat's 110,000. Provider variance is large, so treat volume as an order-of-magnitude guess, not a forecast.

Local demand is a population-scaled proxy. National volume × city population share is not measured Laredo demand. Only the calls can tell you if local intent exists.

Places is a sample, not a census. Repeat queries returned different counts (Laredo septic: 7 vs 10 results). Reviews change daily. Every row here should be re-checked when you call.

Chain classification is heuristic. "City of Laredo" and "Crane Worldwide Logistics" got flagged as chains; they are simply not prospects, which is the outcome that matters.
