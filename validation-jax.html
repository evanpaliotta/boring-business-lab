The pivot this scan forces

read this before calling

Locally, sell intake and capacity — not leads.

In Laredo the interesting thing was invisibility: good operators with 1–2 reviews and no web presence. Jacksonville doesn't have that. It has dozens of competent independents who already rank, already have sites, and whose actual failure mode is nobody answering at 7pm on a Saturday, or a crew booked out while the next guy is idle.

So the two local products are: (1) an after-hours intake desk — one number, real availability, dispatch to whoever can serve today, billed per dispatched job; and (2) surge brokering after a named storm, when availability is the scarce resource and out-of-area crews are calling in.

Why you and not a national platform: you can drive to these shops, you know the beach, and the operators will take a call from someone local. That is the entire moat at this stage — and it is exactly what an $11 Laredo site could never have.

Script, adjusted for a served market

“Hey — I'm local, over in Jacksonville Beach.
I'm not selling leads. I'm trying to figure out
whether the after-hours and overflow problem
here is worth building something for.

Three questions:
1. When a call comes in after hours or on a
   weekend, what happens to it?
2. How often are you booked out when someone
   needs it this week?
3. When you can't take it, where does that
   person go?

If they engage:
4. What would it be worth to have those calls
   answered, qualified, and put in front of you
   ready to book?
5. Who else in town do you trust enough to
   hand work to?”

Never open with “I can get you more leads.” In a served market that reads as spam and you'll be dismissed in ten seconds.

Kill criteria for the local play

decide before dialling

Kill after-hours intake if: 5 of 8 operators say calls already get answered or go to a service they pay for, or nobody will name a price for a booked job, or they all want someone else to pay for the answering and won't pay a per-job rate.

Kill surge brokering if: operators say storm work is handled by insurers and national catastrophe crews (likely for large losses), leaving you only scraps — or you conclude you cannot commit availability fast enough during a surge without a real relationship book.

Keep going if: two or more operators describe the same specific failure (missed after-hours calls, or turning away bookable work) and at least one names a dollar figure — that is your first paid test.

Method & limits

so you know what this is

Source: Google Maps local list view, 8 queries (4 trades × Jacksonville + Jacksonville Beach), captured 22 Oct 2026. Names, phones, ratings and website-presence are as displayed.

Review counts are not included. Maps' list view no longer shows them inline in this layout, so supply strength here rests on operator count, rating, website presence and duplicates — not review depth. That is a real limitation, and one reason the calls matter.

Duplicate and multi-listing operators appear. B & W Marine and McCue each show more than one listing under different numbers; Coker serves both areas. Unique operator counts are de-duplicated in the summary above but not in the area tables.

This is a call list, not a market study. No revenue, no lead volume, no capacity data, no pricing. All of that requires the phone.

Cost note: paid data providers were unavailable for this run (treg balance $0.01; Litescrape returning 503s), so this used free Maps data instead. The Laredo pack used the paid Places API — the two are not perfectly comparable.
